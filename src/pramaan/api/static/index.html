    How it works

    Four checks, in the order a careful junior would do them.

    1. 1

      Find

      Every citation in the draft: neutral (2023 INSC 11), SCR, SCC, AIR and SCC OnLine, with the case name and any quotation beside it. Footnotes in Word files too.

    2. 2

      Prove

      The citation number must lead to exactly one judgment. SCC and AIR numbers count only when at least two other judgments print the same pairing.

    3. 3

      Compare

      Both parties of the name must fit, the year must agree, and each quotation is matched word for word against the judgment's text, down to a dropped "not".

    4. 4

      Report

      One clear status per citation, the reason in a sentence, and the judgment's own words next to yours. Download it as an annexure for the file.

    A machine can raise a flag. It never gives a green tick on a guess. Verified means proven by the number, the name and the text. Anything less is marked for a person to look at.

    What the statuses mean

    Plain words, and never “fake” when we simply do not know.

    Verified

    The number proves the judgment, the name and year match, and any quotation is word for word. It does not say the judgment supports your argument.

    Needs review

    Not proven, or close but not exact: a near-match quote, one party that differs, a match on names alone. A person should look.

    Misquote · Name mismatch · Wrong paragraph

    Said only when the case is proven by its number and the quotation or name is clearly tied to that citation.

    Not in corpus

    No matching judgment in the dataset. Not a finding that it is fake: it may be a High Court case or a reporter we cannot map.

    Scan noise in source

    The quotation matches except for what looks like a scanning error in the judgment's text. Compare with the printed page.

    Cannot verify

    The check could not run, for example because the stored judgment has no text.

    Accuracy

    Measured, published, and still being measured.

    95.8%of SCC/AIR citations matched to the right judgment (667 items)
    0.4%matched to a wrong judgment
    275/275genuine quotations verified word for word

    2 of 1,166 altered quotations still passed as verified; both were read by hand and are explained in the project's notes, with one fixed since. The text of older scanned judgments has errors (about 2% of characters on a 40-page sample we transcribed ourselves, not human-verified), which is why near matches go to review rather than to an accusation.

    Privacy and limits

    Your draft stays yours.

    • Not stored. The file is read into a temporary place, checked and deleted. No account, no history, no tracking, no third-party scripts.
    • No AI on your text by default. The checks are exact comparisons against the judgments. An optional AI second opinion exists, is off on this site, and could only ever lower a result.
    • What it does not check. Whether the judgment supports your argument; High Court and tribunal cases; short forms such as "supra" and "ibid"; headers, footers and text boxes in Word files.
    • Not legal advice. An automated check of citations. Verify against an authorised source. A lawyer decides.
